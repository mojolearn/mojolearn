Neural performance priorities
2026-10-28

1. Repair repeated setup in public Mamba .step.
   The last measured Mamba2 regression was approximately 15x on AMD and
   29x on NVIDIA. Retain execution plans, weight metadata and state buffers
   across calls; refresh what actually changes. Preserve mutation detection,
   ownership and buffer-lifetime checks. The pending typed-transport batch
   helps, but does not prove that all repeated preparation is gone.

2. Keep generation state on the GPU throughout generation.
   Finish the resident greedy/EOS batch. Avoid uploading/exporting the KV
   cache and downloading full logits every token. Target transfers and
   synchronization in the critical loop.

3. Finish integrating allocation-reuse repairs.
   Generation vocabulary-copy removal, reusable token/logit buffers and
   inference scratch reuse have landed. Ensure the public paths benefit;
   inspect remaining sampling and report allocations. Finish the pending
   compiled Mamba, resident-generation and SmallMLP batches after review.

4. Freeze and measure the repaired neural paths before expanding the work.
   When measurements are authorized again, start with the previously
   regressed public Mamba2 .step workload and representative generation
   workloads, then run the agreed 29-lane neural suite. Keep public-step
   and whole-generation results separate: they exercise different overhead.
   Retain required correctness/identity evidence separately from compilation.
   This recommendation does not authorize a measurement campaign now.

Defer further broad Python removal and new arithmetic experiments until
measurements establish recovery. Keep efficient direct CPython calls where
useful; eliminating CPython references is not itself a performance goal.

The strongest concrete targets are less setup, allocation, copying and
synchronization per token. Focused compilations and source review establish
implementation progress, not measured speedups or runtime bitwise identity.
