What this first stage checks
---------------------------
1. Float64 rank-one affine/information scan against a serial time-varying
   filter. The algebra input Q is exactly sigma2 R R^T in float64. T, R,
   sigma2, alpha0 and P0 are shared rounded inputs. No steady-gain freezing.
2. Proposed float32 scan against a float64 reference with the SAME rounded
   model that the float32 serial emulation receives. Production-style Q32
   is not exactly rank one when lifted to float64. Its lost conditional
   covariance is therefore part of this numerical viability gate, not
   hidden in the algebra proof.
3. Forward finite differences at h=2^-10 in Jones-parameter coordinates,
   including mu and sigma2, matching the optimizer's fixed perturbation and
   -LL/(nobs-1) objective. Report effective float32 perturbation (large
   parameters can swallow h). Distinguish math64 gradient consistency from
   float32 candidate gradient errors versus the same serial64 objective.

The baseline is explicitly a NumPy float32 SERIAL EMULATION, not the actual
main binary. Common initialization uses a float64 Lyapunov solve rounded
once; device main's initializer is not being replayed or certified. BLAS,
FMA and tanh rounding can differ. Even a full PASS only supports proceeding
to a device implementation and a quality gate against main's actual binary.

Fixtures: full p/q=0..3 board grid at n=1392, rd<=4; positive/negative near
unit AR and MA roots, paired near-unit MA roots, repeated roots and third
order MA; n=127/255/256/257/1391/1393/1401, scales 1e-3/1/1e3, and constant,
impulse and alternating signals. No claim to cover actual taxi forecasting.
Float64 field tolerance is fixed at 1e-9 + 1e-8 * reference max magnitude;
its finite-difference bound is propagated through h. Float32 field errors
and every gradient component must be no worse than emulation, with ZERO
extra degradation allowance. Record failures; do not loosen these gates.

K1's five-object Gaussian composition is consistent with the full monoid
from which this rank-one four-object specialization is derived:
  G=(I+C_left J_right)^-1, H=(I+J_right C_left)^-1
  A=A_right G A_left
  b=A_right G(b_left+C_left eta_right)+b_right
  C=A_right G C_left A_right^T+C_right
  eta=eta_left+A_left^T H(eta_right-J_right b_left)
  J=J_left+A_left^T H J_right A_left
When both segment C are zero, G=H=I and our reduced operator follows.
K1 carries C through the scan, including t=0 posterior initialization.
The reduced algorithm scans later leaves and applies the initial posterior
afterward; compare identical chronological prefixes, not just terminal LL.

Source review observations:
* K1 leaves compute C = P - (PZ)(PZ)^T/(ZPZ). This assumes symmetric P.
  Device-rounded Q and stationary P0 can have small asymmetry; test that
  convention against main, and do not mistake algebraic symmetry for
  bit-level equivalence. The two separate compose inverses deliberately
  avoid assuming rounded C and J remain symmetric.
* A pivot magnitude >=1e-20 is not a condition-number or solve-residual
  guarantee. Matrix solves need residual/quality checks, not only finite
  pivots. The source correctly refuses rather than adding jitter.
* A full scan includes the initial posterior leaf (A0=0, b0=m0, C0=C0).
  Ping-pong parity, one-observation inputs and K2's final partial block
  require explicit adapters and checks; no adapter exists in this intake.
* kalman_scan_likelihood loops over nobs on a single thread. This is a
  deliberate source control, but violates the parallel GPU default rule.
  It must remain experimental; a validated multiblock reduction is required
  before any of K1/K2/K3 becomes a production default.
* Scalar/final-state kernels also dispatch one writer; the production
  adapter should parallelize across models/state entries rather than add
  per-model tiny launches. K2 has bounded local chains of B observations
  and needs a genuinely multi-block launch over time/model blocks.
