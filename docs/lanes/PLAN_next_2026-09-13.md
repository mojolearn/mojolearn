## 1. GEMM (highest value, start here)

**Why.** Neural is our worst domain: the LM step is 3.78x torch eager-fp32 and
11.90x torch compile_bf16. GEMM is **145 ms of the step, 48%**; attention is
116 ms, 39%; nothing else exceeds 4.3 ms.

**The ceiling, know it before renting.** Closing GEMM to parity would take most
of the eager-fp32 gap. It CANNOT close the compile_bf16 gap: even a GEMM at
zero leaves us ~8x, because that gap is mostly bf16-against-our-fp32 plus their
fused kernels. Do not promise the headline number.

**Know the size of what remains before starting it.** The one compiled kernel
(`identical_gemm_tuned_kernel[8,8,16,16,16,PAGES]`, 255 registers, one block per
SM, ~76.5 GFLOP/s per block) is the whole story now. torch 2.4.1 eager FP32 runs
the ENTIRE step in 62 ms on the same pod; our GEMM alone is 141 ms.

**The ceiling is 33.5 TFLOP/s, not 67 (corrected 2026-09-13).** The identity
contract's per-step seam on NVIDIA is TWO issued instructions, `fma.rn` then
`mul.rn.ftz` by one; the single `fma.rn.ftz` was measured WRONG at the
smallest-normal boundary (262,144 adversarial triples on L40S and H100,
`docs/lanes/HANDOFF_performance_followup_2026-09-09.md`). The H100 issues one
warp instruction per cycle per scheduler whatever the pipe, so two instructions
per product step halves the usable FMA peak: 33.5 TFLOP/s at 1.98 GHz
(`BRIEF_gemm_kernel_2026-09-11.md` section 3.2). The shipped 11.4 is 34% of
that, not 15%. Bounds, on the 142 ms GEMM sum: contract ceiling 45 ms; a clean
SIMT rewrite at 60 to 70% of it, 65 to 76 ms; cuBLAS SGEMM at one instruction
per step, 28 to 34 ms, unreachable under this contract by construction. So a
rewrite is a 2x, not a 4x, and "cuBLAS-competitive" is the wrong target. The
only lever on the ceiling itself is the seam, a CONTRACT question (do the three
columns' native flush-to-zero FMAs agree with one another at the boundary?),
and that probe has not been run. GEMM is also not the whole gap: at its contract
ceiling it saves 97 ms of the 280 ms to torch; attention (116 ms) and glue (85
ms) are the larger half afterwards.

**Before any rewrite: `kpack_pad` (DEVIATION 2700, branch `lane/gemm-kpack-pad`,
brief section 13).** The `kpack` arm's packed B page put all 16 column threads
of a warp on ONE bank group (group stride 128 words, `0 mod 32`), an eight-way
conflict on every per-step load; section 12's "a shared load per step costs
more than the copies" reading skipped that count. `kpack_pad` pads the stride
to 132 and changes nothing else. **MEASURED 2026-09-13 on an H100
(`bench/results/e1g/2026-09-13_150005-nvidia-h100-gemm-kpack-pad`): the conflict
was the whole `kpack` loss.** Same pod, same commit: `kpack` 1.308 on the lean
step, `kpack_pad` 1.014, every witness equal; GEMM sum 1.50 against 1.02. NO
FLIP, but the reading changes: the per-window register copies and a per-step
shared load cost the same within a few percent, so neither is the lever. An
extra load per step hid completely, which an issue-bound kernel could not
absorb, so the block reads LATENCY-bound at two warps per scheduler. The next
arm is a second block per SM, which needs at most 128 registers, and the 2540
lines say accumulator count alone does not get there (16 cells read 214). Where
the other ~100 registers go is a resources question to answer before any arm
(brief section 13.5).

**Recommendation.** Priority 2, which is unowned, local, and has a known
mechanism. Then the register census above (one resources build, no LM run).
Open the rewrite only against the 33.5 ceiling, with its size stated up front.

**Frame it correctly or not at all.** This is a USER-FACING improvement: a user

exists to prevent. In-tree precedent: `_ResidentForest` in

**Estimate 1-2 days**, dominated by the invalidation gate and a cross-vendor
identity re-check, not by the caching. A cached upload that goes stale silently

## 4. DBSCAN: `rbc` is a low-dimension default

Measured, both ends on one board: **0.084x at d=11** (12x faster than cuML) and
**6.121x at d=220** (6x slower) -- a swing of 73x, same parameters, same agreed
clustering (ARI 0.9999999991 and 0.9999993801). The random ball cover's pruning
degrades as dimension rises until the cover stops excluding candidates; cuML's
brute force has no such cliff. Gating the default on dimension helps wide data
without touching narrow data. This is the DBSCAN lane's to act on; the sweep
deliberately moved no default.

## Working rules that cost real time today

- **Check `git rev-parse --abbrev-ref HEAD` immediately before EVERY commit.**
  A commit landed on another lane's branch because that lane checked out mid-task,
  and the following `git push origin main` said "Everything up-to-date" while
  publishing nothing. Both commands succeeded.
- **A probe needs a control.** Four verification greps were mis-aimed or
  non-discriminating: a phrase split across two source lines returns 0 on the
  broken side too; `grep -c` counts LINES not occurrences; `git grep -l | wc -l`
  counts FILENAMES; and in zsh `for x in $VAR` does NOT word-split, so a path
  list collapses to one nonexistent filename and every check returns 0. Run the
  probe against the UNFIXED side and watch it FAIL first.
- **An A/B whose two builds hash the same is a binary compared against itself.**
  Report INERT, never "bits unchanged".
- **Never reap a box while a run is owed**, and write the pod id to
  `bench/results/runpod_leases/` so an orphan is findable outside one agent's
  context.
- **R2 first**: `tools/dataset_store.sh stage` -- per-pod setup is 2 min instead
  of 21 because the istella npz is stored DECODED.
